The Old Library

The old library on the corner of Maple Street was the best place in the whole town. Emma loved it. She went there every Saturday morning when the bells of the church down the road were still ringing. She liked the sound of the bells. She liked the smell of the books too. They smelled like something good and old and safe.

The librarian was a small woman with white hair and very round glasses. Her name was Mrs. Chen. She knew where every single book was. If Emma asked for a book about horses, or stars, or secret gardens, Mrs. Chen would smile and walk straight to the right shelf. She never had to look it up. She just knew.

One cold Saturday in December, Emma came to the library as usual. The sky was grey and the wind was sharp. She pushed open the heavy wooden door and felt the warm air of the library wrap around her like a blanket. She shook the rain off her coat and went to the children's section at the back.

There was a new boy sitting at the big round table. He had dark hair and a red scarf and he was reading a very large book. Emma had never seen him before. She sat down at the other end of the table and opened her own book. But she kept looking at the boy over the top of her pages.

After a while the boy looked up. He had brown eyes. He did not smile right away. Then he did.

Hello, he said. I am Marco. We just moved here from Milan.

Emma did not know where Milan was. She would look it up later in the atlas. I am Emma, she said. I come here every Saturday.

It is a good library, Marco said. He looked around at all the shelves. Very good. At home I had a library on my street too. I miss it.

Emma thought about this. She had never missed a library because she had always had this one. She felt a little sad for Marco and also a little happy that the library was good enough for him to like it.

Do you want me to show you the best books? she asked.

Marco smiled a bigger smile this time. Yes please, he said.

So Emma showed him the shelf with the adventure stories, and the shelf with the nature books, and the very high shelf at the back that had old maps of places that did not exist any more. Marco liked the maps the most. He pulled one out carefully and spread it on the table. His eyes went wide.

Look, he said. This is older than my grandmother.

They sat at the big round table for the rest of the morning, looking at old maps and talking about the places on them. Mrs. Chen brought them each a cup of hot cocoa at eleven o'clock, which she sometimes did for the children who stayed a long time. The cocoa was sweet and warm. The rain kept falling outside.

When the bells rang twelve times Emma knew she had to go home for lunch. She put on her coat. Marco put on his red scarf.

Will you come next Saturday? Emma asked.

Yes, Marco said. I will come every Saturday.

Emma smiled. Good, she said.

She pushed open the heavy wooden door and walked out into the cold grey street. But she felt warm all the way home. The library had given her something good today. It had given her, along with all its books and maps and quiet and cocoa, a friend.
