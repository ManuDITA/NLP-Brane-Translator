\chapter{Evaluation}\label{s:evaluation}

This chapter reports the empirical results used to answer RQ1--RQ4
(Chapter~\ref{s:intro}). All experiments use the metrics defined in
Chapter~\ref{s:implementation}: \emph{compile rate}, \emph{execution rate},
and \emph{output match rate}.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Dataset.} 584 unique intent--BraneScript pairs (deduplicated from
1316 raw examples authored across nine source files, after removing 74
duplicates using a fixed file-priority order, Chapter~\ref{s:design}), split
into 497 training and 87 validation examples. An additional 1974 paraphrases
(three per intent) were generated for the semantic cache evaluation
(Section~\ref{s:eval-cache}).

\textbf{Models.} Three base models are evaluated: Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.5-9B, and
Qwen3.6-27B, all served without fine-tuning as a baseline, and with LoRA-based
supervised fine-tuning (Chapter~\ref{s:design}) for the two larger sizes.

\textbf{Hardware.} All generation and training runs execute on Snellius
(Dutch national HPC facility) H100 GPU nodes, submitted via SLURM.

\section{RQ1/RQ2: Translation Accuracy and the Effect of Retrieval and Prompt Engineering Quality}

An initial evaluation round (27 July) established baseline compile/execution
rates for all three base models with an early version of the system prompt and
retrieval configuration:

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Execution rate} & \textbf{Output match} & \textbf{Comparable $n$} \\
\hline
Qwen3.6-27B (base) & 37.9\% & 40.3\% & 196 \\
Qwen3.5-9B (base)  & 20.9\% & 28.0\% & 107 \\
Qwen3.5-4B (base)  & 5.5\%  & 58.1\% & 31 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Baseline execution and output-match rates, 27 July evaluation round.}
\label{tab:baseline-july}
\end{table}

After diagnosing several infrastructure issues --- an system prompt that
permitted empty-string arguments and under-specified \texttt{commit\_result}
semantics, insufficiently precise RAG package aliases, and generation output
that was not being cleaned of stray formatting tokens --- the same three base
models were re-evaluated with a corrected prompt and retrieval configuration
(1 August), without any change to model weights:

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Compile rate} & \textbf{Execution rate} & \textbf{Output match} \\
\hline
Qwen3.6-27B (base) & 94.8\% & 92.7\% & 56.5\% \\
Qwen3.5-9B (base)  & 64.5\% & 63.4\% & --- \\
Qwen3.5-4B (base)  & 57.0\% & 55.4\% & --- \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Baseline compile/execution rates after prompt and RAG fixes, 1 August evaluation round.}
\label{tab:baseline-august}
\end{table}

The execution rate of the 27B model rises from 37.9\% to 92.7\% between the
two rounds, using identical model weights. This is the central finding for
RQ1/RQ2: for this task, the combined quality of retrieval context and prompt
correctness has a larger effect on translation accuracy than model scale, and
a meaningful fraction of what initially appears as a model-capability
limitation is in fact an engineering and grounding problem. Note that this
comparison changes several factors at once --- RAG package aliases, the system
prompt, and output post-processing all improved together between the two
rounds --- so it demonstrates that retrieval-and-prompt quality \emph{as a
whole} matters more than model scale, but it does not isolate the specific
contribution of RAG alone against a true RAG-disabled baseline; no such
ablation was run in this thesis (Chapter~\ref{sec:threats}). This still directly
supports the addressal of gap G2 (Chapter~\ref{s:background}): providing
precise, package-grounded context is necessary for a general-purpose LLM to
reliably target a DSL absent from its pre-training data.

\section{RQ3: Effect of Supervised Fine-Tuning}

A first SFT model (Qwen3.5-9B, fine-tuned on the pre-deduplication 560/98
split with the earlier system prompt) was evaluated using the corrected
(1~August) evaluation prompt, and achieved 56.8\% compile rate and 49.5\%
execution rate --- a regression relative to the corresponding 63.4\% execution
rate of the un-tuned base model under the same prompt. The cause was traced to
a mismatch between the prompt format used during training and the prompt
format used at evaluation time: the model had specialised to the training-time
prompt's conventions, which no longer matched the corrected evaluation prompt.
This is itself a substantive finding for RQ3: fine-tuning gains are contingent
on strict consistency between the training and inference prompt format, and a
prompt change invalidates previously fine-tuned models unless they are
retrained against the new prompt.

Following the dataset deduplication (497/87 split, Section~\ref{s:design}) and
the prompt fix, two new LoRA fine-tunes were trained for five epochs each
(\texttt{qwen3.5-9b-ep5}, \texttt{qwen3.6-27b-ep5}) against the corrected
prompt.

\section{RQ4: Semantic Cache as a Reproducibility Layer}\label{s:eval-cache}

The semantic cache was seeded with all 584 unique training intents and their
reference BraneScript, then queried with the 1974 paraphrases across eight
candidate similarity thresholds (Chapter~\ref{s:implementation}).

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
\textbf{Threshold} & \textbf{Hit rate} & \textbf{Correct rate} & \textbf{False-positive rate} \\
\hline
0.80 & 100.0\% & 99.1\% & 0.9\% \\
0.85 & 100.0\% & 99.1\% & 0.9\% \\
0.88 & 99.9\%  & 99.0\% & 0.9\% \\
0.90 & 99.7\%  & 98.9\% & 0.9\% \\
0.92 (default) & 99.1\% & 98.3\% & 0.8\% \\
0.94 & 96.2\%  & 95.4\% & 0.8\% \\
0.96 & 88.1\%  & 87.5\% & 0.6\% \\
0.98 & 59.1\%  & 59.1\% & 0.1\% \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Semantic cache threshold sweep over 1974 paraphrased intents (2 August evaluation round).}
\label{tab:cache-sweep}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{cache_sweep.png}
    \caption{Cache hit rate and correctness as a function of the similarity threshold.}
    \label{fig:cache-sweep}
\end{figure}

Two observations follow directly from Table~\ref{tab:cache-sweep}. First, the
false-positive rate never drops below approximately 0.9\% regardless of how
conservative the threshold is set, until the threshold becomes so high
(0.98) that hit rate collapses to 59.1\%; lowering the threshold below the
default 0.92 (down to 0.88) yields a strictly better hit rate at effectively
the same false-positive cost. Second, manual inspection of the residual false
positives shows they are dominated by intent pairs that differ only in a
specific parameter value (e.g., a numeric threshold such as ``per 100,000''
vs.\ ``per 200,000'', or a swapped column name such as ``height'' vs.\
``weight''), which the \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} embedding model places at
cosine similarity above 0.97 because it encodes propositional meaning rather
than exact parameter values. This is a direct empirical illustration of the
tension noted in Chapter~\ref{s:background}: no similarity threshold can
eliminate this class of false positive without also discarding a large
fraction of valid hits, because the two failure modes share the same
underlying cause. This result is discussed further, together with its
implications for gap G3, in Chapter~\ref{s:discussion}.
